## D1. Which length-scale variable to carry; advect/remap k and ψ or re-initialise?

### What the papers say
- **All two-equation models are one model.** UB03 write the second equation for a generic ψ = (c0μ)^p k^m l^n (their Eq. 5, Table 1) and show that models differing only in the exponents (p, m, n) behave identically once the constants are chosen to satisfy the constraints (decay of homogeneous turbulence, log layer, mixing layer, steady-state Ri) (UB03 l.1262-1318, Table 7, Fig. 2). K18 says the same in one sentence: "when properly calibrated, all these two-equation models which differ only in the turbulent transport term perform similarly" (K18 l.680-684). W05 confirm in ROMS with k-kl, k-ε, k-ω and "gen" (W05 l.724, Table 1 l.403-475).
- **Variable choice matters for numerics, not accuracy.** UB03 l.876: models with positive n (k-kl, n = 1) *require* a wall function to keep the ψ diffusion term positive (W05 l.707-726, Eq. 28-29: σψ becomes negative unless c2 F_wall > c1). Models with n < 0 (k-ε: n = -1; k-ω: n = -1; gen: n = -0.67) do not. For a model with layers that move and thin, the wall-function-free variants are the safe ones; k-ε is also what Burchard/Klingbeil/GETM use (BR08 l.486-488).
- **Parameter sets** (W05 Table 1, l.403-475; UB03 Table 2, Table 7):
  - k-ε: ψ = ε = (c0μ)³ k^1.5 l^-1, p = 3, m = 1.5, n = -1, σk = 1.0, σψ = 1.3, c1 = 1.44, c2 = 1.92, c3+ = 1.0 (unstable), c3- = -0.52 (Kantha-Clayson SF), -0.63 (Canuto A), -0.57 (Canuto B) (W05 Table 2, l.476-516; UB03 Table 8 gives -0.629 for R87 + Canuto ASM with Ri_st = 0.25), c0μ = 0.5477 for Canuto-type SF in UB03 Table 2 (W05 uses c0μ from the stability function: KC 0.5544, CA 0.5270, CB 0.5540, Table 2 header), F_wall = 1.
  - generic (UB03 Table 7 line 1): p = 2, m = 1, n = -0.67, σk = 0.8, σψ = 1.07, c1 = 1, c2 = 1.22, c3- = 0.05 (CA), no wall function (W05 l.688-690, Table 1).
- **Dissipation from the length scale**: ε = c_ε k^(3/2)/l (K18 Eq. 25, l.640-651); K = S k^(1/2) l (K18 Eq. 24).
- **Moving layers: what exists.**
  - H10 (the only paper here that treats adaptive layers) does *not* discuss how k and ε are carried when layers move. It states only that turbulence comes from GOTM inside GETM and that the model-loop order is grid adaptation → grid velocities → "Evaluating the turbulent mixing quantities" → T/S update (H10 l.840-861). The tracers (and transports) are moved "by the chosen advection scheme based on the grid-related vertical velocities" with a high-order scheme, "a specific remapping ... is not necessary" (H10 l.858-864). Nothing is said about k, ε; whether GETM advects them with the grid velocity is **[verificar]**.
  - Hal04 (HYCOM, hybrid layers): for Mellor-Yamada 2.5, q² and q²l are "carried as layer variables in all HYCOM routines" (advected, diffused, relocated with the interfaces by the hybrid-grid generator) and passed as *interface* variables to the 1-D solver, because the M-Y interfaces are put at the mid-depths of the HYCOM layers; "fluxes of q² and q²l across model interfaces relocated by the hybrid grid generator are also calculated" (Hal04 l.366-396, Fig. 2). HYCOM source details: `tke_in_ale.md` §1 (layer-content filtering, PLM/PCM remap).
- **Burchard & Rennau 2008 / Klingbeil 2014 / Burchard 2012** say nothing about numerical mixing of k and ε themselves; they quantify numerical mixing of tracers and momentum and compare to the physical TKE dissipation (BR08 l.795-808; Burchard 2012 l.99-102). Useful consequence: the *physical* mixing from the closure is what the variance-decay diagnostic in `diagnostics/mixing.py` should be compared against (H10 Eq. 35: D_phys = 2 K_v (∂z s)², l.880-890).

### Recommendation
1. Carry **k and ε** (k-ε, UB03/W05 constants above) as the primary pair; compute l = (c0μ)³ k^1.5/ε only for limiting. This keeps n < 0 (no wall function) and ε is the quantity whose floor is physical. The alternative "gen" (p = 2, m = 1, n = -0.67) is equally valid and has a smaller dynamic range of ψ.
2. Treat k and ε as thickness-weighted scalars: carry h·k and h·ε through the Lagrangian step and remap, as HYCOM does with q², q²l (Hal04). This is the only published precedent for ALE; the choice of whether to *advect and remap* versus *re-initialise from the local balance each step* is not settled in these papers. Physical argument (design judgement, not literature): the k adjustment time ~ k/ε is minutes in a boundary layer and hours in the thermocline, whereas an ALE regrid moves interfaces by a fraction of a layer per step; a re-initialisation would erase the memory that K18 l.3079-3083 and K18 l.583-586 argue is first-order in coastal flows (tidal slack/flood). So remap, but with positivity-preserving PCM/PLM and the floors of D2 applied after the remap.
3. Because k and ε live on interfaces (D4), remap them on the dual grid whose cells are bounded by layer centres (HYCOM's inverse trick, Hal04 Fig. 2), or equivalently remap layer-mean values of the interface quantity averaged to centres and re-interpolate back; either is consistent with the finite-volume content. Which to prefer is a design choice (not in the papers).
4. After remap re-apply the Galperin bound on ψ (D2) so a remapped (k, ε) pair cannot violate l ≤ l_max.

## D2. Positivity: Patankar vs clipping; floors; Galperin limit

- **Patankar trick (source linearisation)**. K18 §7.6-7.7, l.3052-3087: explicit discretisation of the TKE equation "might require very small time steps during slack tide, when production is small and dissipation is high, in order to guarantee positivity of k"; the semi-implicit treatment of the loss term, Eq. (74) there (the same one used for the quadratic bottom stress), "is used in almost all discretisations of turbulence closure budget equations, including those for ... the dissipation rate ε and the turbulence frequency ω, see Deleersnijder et al. (1997) and Burchard et al. (2005)". Form (Patankar 1980, Sec. 7.2-2; applied to k-ε in Burchard & Baumert 1995 / GOTM **[verificar]**): write the sink as ε = (ε/k) k, treat it as `-(ε^n/k^n) k^(n+1)` in the implicit tridiagonal diagonal, and keep the source P + B explicit; if B < 0 (stable) move the part B into the implicit diagonal too: `B^n (k^(n+1)/k^n)`. For ε the analogous: `c2 ε^(n+1) ε^n/k^n` implicit; source `c1 (ε/k)(P) + c3 (ε/k) B^+`; negative B^- goes into the diagonal. Rule (K18 sign convention): the *positive* contributions explicit, *negative* ones implicit, so the matrix is an M-matrix and k, ε stay positive without clipping.
- **Floors still needed**: W05 Table 5 (l.1260-1280) applies, after the tridiagonal solve, `k = max(k, k_min)`, `ψ = max(ψ, ψ_min)` and the Galperin limit on ψ. Values (W05 Table 1, l.440-444; the column assignment is uncertain in the OCR so confirm): k_min = 5.0e-6 for k-kl, 7.6e-6 m² s⁻² for k-ε / k-ω / gen; ψ_min = 1e-8 for k-kl and 1e-12 for k-ε / k-ω / gen. Also K18 l.656-658: the TKE floor "to avoid unrealistically low diapycnal diffusivities in the thermocline, unresolved TKE production due to internal wave breaking is parameterised by a prescribed minimum TKE (Bolding et al. 2002)". So k_min is both a numerical and a physical (background mixing) parameter; this matters for the IW goal of stage E.
- **Galperin limit** (Galperin et al. 1988; W05 Eq. 41-43, l.985-1003): the turbulent time scale k/ε must not exceed the buoyancy time scale, which gives l ≤ l_max = 0.56 √k/N (equivalent: G_h = l²N²/(2k) ≥ G_h,min = -0.28... in W05's text "G_h min = 0.28", the sign convention in the OCR is lost; the limit that is stable is |G_h| ≤ 0.28 for stable N²>0). In ψ form, for n < 0 it is a *lower* bound on ψ; for n > 0 an upper bound (Table 5 gives both forms: `ψ = max{ψ, (0.56)^(n/2)... (c0μ)^p k^(m+n/2) N^(-n)}` for n < 0). W05 stress the limit must be used **consistently** in G_h, S_M, S_H, K_M, K_H and in the dissipation (W05 l.1004-1012), not only in some places as in POM/ECOMSED. UB03 Table 7 / W05: with the "right" c3-, the limit is automatically satisfied for some models and not needed (W05 l.1002-1003: "this limitation is not required if the proper c3- value is selected"); keep it anyway as a safety.
- **Kantha-Clayson G_h upper limit** G_h0 = 0.028 to keep the velocity variances positive (W05 l.771-773) — only needed with the quasi-equilibrium KC stability functions, which are the ones used for k-ε in W05's runs. Canuto A/B are the better-posed alternatives (W05 Table 4); `physics/mixing/gls.py` already uses Canuto A (CROCO).
- **Not found in the local text**: any recommendation of clipping *instead of* Patankar. All sources use Patankar for the transport/implicit step and floors as a safety net.

Recommendation: Patankar-linearised tridiagonal solve for k and ε (+ the Galperin bound on ψ and the two floors afterwards); count (diagnostically) how often a floor/limit fires, as a regression test for the ALE remap of D1.

## D3. Shear and buoyancy production, energy-conserving form

- **Principle** (K18 l.2937-2943, l.3573-3576; K14 l.470-475): the kinetic energy removed from the mean flow by the vertical stress divergence must equal the shear production P added to TKE, and the potential energy exchange must equal B, *also in the discrete equations*; "Burchard (2002b) shows how a consistent energy exchange can be obtained also on the numerical level". K14 l.472-475 describe the method: "In the context of a conservative discretisation of the production term for turbulent kinetic energy, Burchard (2002) presented a closed derivation ... the discrete diffusive fluxes of second moment" are deduced from the *first-moment* (momentum) transport equation, as in the Poisson-equation principle: the second equation must be *derived from* the first, not discretised independently.
- **The stencil** (not in the local text; **[verificar]** against Burchard 2002, Lecture Notes "Applied turbulence modelling in marine waters" and GOTM `production.F90`): in a layered model with the implicit viscous step `h_k (u_k^{n+1} - u_k^*)/Δt = [ν_{k+1/2}(u_{k+1}^{n+1} - u_k^{n+1})/Δz_{k+1/2} - ν_{k-1/2}(u_k^{n+1} - u_{k-1}^{n+1})/Δz_{k-1/2}]`, multiplying by u_k^{*}... gives the discrete kinetic-energy sink; the exact counterpart per interface is the product of the **new-time and old-time** shear: `P_{k+1/2} = ν_{k+1/2} (u_{k+1}^{n+1} - u_k^{n+1})(u_{k+1}^{*} - u_k^{*})/Δz_{k+1/2}²` (same for v), with the *same* ν and Δz that went into the implicit viscous step, and similarly `B_{k+1/2} = -ν'_{k+1/2} N²_{k+1/2}` with N² formed from the *same* stratification that the tracer solve uses. In GOTM/GETM this is why the viscous step is done **before** the turbulence step: u^{n+1} is available (GOTM's `shear_frequency` with `uo`, `u`). Whether the product is (old·new) or (new·new) depends on version; **[verificar]**.
- **ROMS/W05 does not do this**: W05 Table 5 (l.1260-1280): "P = K_M M², B = K_H N²" with the *old* K_M, M², N² (before the new velocities/density are available), then the tridiagonal solve for k and ψ. So the two published implementations differ; W05 report it works, and K18 treats the energy-consistent form as an improvement, not a necessity.
- **Does it matter for stability?** Not stated in the local papers as a stability condition; the argument is energetic consistency (K18 l.3573: it is one of the sources of "energy-conservation violation"). In the flow regimes where the closure is slave to a strongly changing shear (tidal slack, internal-wave critical layers) a mismatch of P by O(1) over a step is possible when K_M changes by more than a few tens of percent per step (my argument, not the papers'). Practical rule that follows from the Patankar form: because P is an explicit source for k, a P computed from the *new* shear (u^{n+1}) times the old shear is bounded by the implicit viscous step (it cannot exceed what the viscous step actually removed), which is the stabilising property.
- **ALE complication**: in the ALE mode, momentum is advanced as thickness-weighted h·u in the Lagrangian step; the viscous implicit step, M² and the energy sink must all use the same layer thicknesses (post-Lagrangian, pre-remap) so the identity holds layer by layer. After the remap, P and B must be evaluated on whichever grid k, ε are advanced on. Not addressed in any local paper.
- **M² and N²**: M² = (∂u/∂z)² + (∂v/∂z)², N² = -(g/ρ0) ∂ρ/∂z (UB03 Eq. 9, l.322-331; W05 uses the same). For TEOS-10/in-situ density use the local-pressure density difference N² = -g (ρ_{k+1}(p_{k+1/2}) - ρ_k(p_{k+1/2}))/(ρ0 Δz) **[verificar]** (HYCOM does this, `tke_in_ale.md` §1.2.3).

## D4. Location of k, ε; S², N² on non-uniform / thin layers

- **Location**: K18 l.3071-3076: "On a Lorenz grid discrete turbulent quantities are best located at w-points, i.e. vertically between the tracer points of adjacent layers. Although this offers the prescription of Dirichlet boundary conditions for k and ε directly at the bottom and the free surface, Burchard and Petersen (1999) and Burchard et al. (2005) demonstrated that equivalent Neumann boundary conditions provide much higher accuracy in reproducing the law of the wall." ROMS (W05) does the same: k, ψ and K_M, K_H on w-levels, flux (no-flux Neumann for k, Eq. 52; ∂ψ/∂z flux Eq. 54) boundary conditions; the Dirichlet values (Eq. 51 k = u*²/(c0μ)², Eq. 53 ψ = (c0μ)^(p-2m) k^m (κ z)^n u*^2m...) are only output (W05 l.1165-1235). HYCOM-MY is the exception: k at layer centres on a staggered grid (Hal04 l.378-395).
- **Boundary conditions** (W05 l.1187-1235, Eq. 51-54): k_b = u*_b²/(c0μ)²; ψ_b = (c0μ)^(p-2m) k_b^m (κ z_b)^n with z_b = z0b + half the first-layer thickness (W05 l.1138-1150); applied in flux form: K_M/σk ∂k/∂z = 0 and K_M/σψ ∂ψ/∂z = (K_M/σψ) n (c0μ)^p k^m κ^n z^(n-1) (Eq. 54). The surface roughness z0s is also needed (breaking-wave injection is a separate option, K18 l.664).
- **Non-uniform / thin layers**: The only statement in the local papers is for the stress treatment: bottom-layer thickness enters the drag through ln((h/2 + z0)/z0) (K18 Eq. 72, l.2950-2960) and with h → 0 gives c_D → ∞ (no-slip). L94 Appendix D uses unequal layers directly: gradients at interfaces ∂zX|_{n+1/2} = (X_n - X_{n+1})/Δ_{n+1/2}, with Δ_{n+1/2} the centre-to-centre distance (D4a, l.2590-2605), and gradients at layer centres by the 3-point non-uniform formula (D4b). Same convention for the closure inputs: M², N² at interfaces = difference of the two layer-centre values over the centre-to-centre distance.
- **Thin layers**: nothing in K18/W05/UB03/H10 beyond "positive layer heights" (H10 l.696 requires a minimum layer height in the grid adaptation) and the closure being "consistently" limited. HYCOM restricts the M-Y column to layers thicker than 10 cm (1 mm) (`tke_in_ale.md` §1.2.3a). Design implication for pyOCEAN: exclude vanished/thin layers (h < h_min) from the closure column and fill their k, ε by the neighbouring value (or floors), otherwise Δz_{k+1/2} → 0 makes M², N² blow up and the implicit tridiagonal system very stiff.
- **Adaptive coordinates tied to turbulence variables?** H10 do *not* use TKE or ε to adapt the grid. Their grid adaptation is driven by N² and S² (and distances to surface/bottom) through the grid diffusion coefficients: K_grid = D_grid(c_N2 / N² ... ), with weights c_N2 + c_S2 + c_d + c_b = 1 (H10 Eq. 7-10, l.395-430 and l.825-851): stratification-weighted and shear-weighted components that concentrate layer interfaces where N² and shear are large. So, in the one paper that couples adaptivity to flow state, the quantities are N² and M², not k, ε. K18 l.1185-1218 repeats this summary.

## D5. KPP on non-uniform / moving layers

All from L94 unless stated.

- **Boundary-layer depth** (Sec. 3, l.653-735; Eq. 21-24): h = the smallest depth d at which the bulk Richardson number
  `Ri_b(d) = (B_r - B(d)) d / (|V_r - V(d)|² + V_t²(d))` equals Ri_c. B_r, V_r are **averages over the surface layer 0 < d < ε h** (ε = 0.1), "so the direct use of resolution dependent grid-level values is avoided" (l.680-690); not the first-level values. The unresolved turbulent-shear term (Eq. 22-23, l.694-725):
  `V_t²(d) = C_v (-β_T)^(1/2) / (Ri_c κ²) (c_s ε)^(-1/2) d N w_s`,
  with β_T = -0.2 (entrainment-flux ratio), κ = 0.4, c_s = 98.96, C_v between 1.5 and 1.6 to obtain β_T = -0.2 (Table 1, l.926-950; Table 1: C_v = 1.4/1.6/1.8 gives β_T = -0.171/-0.206/-0.243, independent of Ri_c). Without V_t² the entrainment flux depends on N (l.665-672). Stable forcing: h additionally limited by the Monin-Obukhov length L and the Ekman depth h_E = 0.7 u*/f (Eq. 24, l.700-703). (The note at the end of Appendix C says removing these two limits changed neither SST nor h_m appreciably, l.2549-2553.)
- **Ri_c and resolution sensitivity** (Sec. 5, l.1095-1105, Table 2-3 and Appendix C): Ri_c = 0.3 as the standard "at all resolutions" (0.25 at Δz = 1 m, rising "to about 0.4 as the resolution is degraded to Δz = 5 m"); h_max (Ocean Storms mixed-layer depth after 0.5 inertial periods) changes by more than a third as Ri_c goes 0.25 → 0.75; lower resolution always decreases h_max and larger C_v partly compensates (l.1030-1040, 1097-1104). The KPP deepening has the Pollard et al. scaling h ∝ u*(t/N)^(1/2) with limit u*/(N f)^(1/2) (l.1020-1030). The annual OWS Papa run is "relatively insensitive to an order of magnitude change in vertical resolution" once the Appendix-D numerics are used (l.2255-2264).
- **The discrete-Ri bias and the fix** (Appendix C, l.2290-2350; Appendix D "Interior-boundary layer matching", l.2610-2700): with coarse layers the linearly interpolated discrete Ri_b profile gives h_d systematically below the continuous-profile h_c (Fig. C1-C2) and oscillates as h passes layer interfaces. L94 tried non-linear interpolation, found it not robust ("no one scheme could be found to work well over a wide variety of naturally occurring conditions"), and used a practical fix:
  1. compute the interior diffusivity ν_x(h) and its gradient at the (interpolated) h by the continuous formulas D5a-b;
  2. determine the shape-function coefficients a2, a3 from matching (Eq. 17-18) so K_x(σ) is continuous at σ = 1;
  3. **enhance** the diffusivity applied at the interface k - 1/2 (the one immediately above the layer containing h, Fig. D1-D2): Λ_x = weighted average of the interior ν_x(d_{k-1/2}) and an "enhanced" boundary-layer diffusivity K'_x, with weight R = (h - d_{k-1/2})/Δ (D6, l.2690-2720), so mixing acts on a gradient even when h is between interface k-1/2 and layer centre k; "the effect: the discrete scheme removes the bias and dampens the oscillation".
  4. Solve the semi-implicit diffusion `A^{i+1} X^{t+1} = X^t + H^i` (D7) and **iterate** until |h^i - h^{i+1}|/Δ_a < tolerance; ≥ 2 iterations always; on the first, X^t or a linear extrapolation is used; over an OWS Papa year < 1 % of the steps needed more than two, max 12, 0.3 % of iterations failed to converge in weak stratification and the 20th was used (l.2759-2805). Hal04 (HYCOM): "two iterations are generally sufficient" (Hal04 l.324-337).
- **Matching to the interior**: ν_x(h) and ∂zν_x(h) of the interior (shear instability ν0 = 50e-4 m² s⁻¹, Ri_0 = 0.7, p = 3 (Eq. 28); internal-wave background ν_m = 1e-4, ν_s = 0.1e-4 m² s⁻¹ (Eq. 29); double diffusion) force G(1), ∂σG(1) so fluxes, time rates and gradients are continuous up to second order (Eq. 18, l.640-652). The CVMix practice **no matching** (van Roekel 2018: `MatchTechnique`) is not in the local text; **[verificar]**: simple matching (K_x continuous at h only) was the CVMix default and van Roekel et al. recommended `ParabolicNonLocal` ... with resolution-dependent warnings; MOM6 notes in `kpp_mom6_cvmix.md`. In Adcroft et al. (2019, OM4, l.503-506) the GFDL group abandoned KPP with z* because of "implementation choices when employing KPP and then missing features in the package (Van Roekel et al. 2018)" and moved to ePBL: a caution for the pyOCEAN implementation.
- **Nonlocal term** (Eq. 19-20, l.607-636): γ_s = C_s (w_x(0) + wθ_R...)/(w_s(σ) h) for scalars in unstable forcing only (ζ < 0 → 0 otherwise); γ_m = 0 (momentum nonlocal transport "not parameterised"). C* = 10 in the highly convective case; C_s = C* κ (c_s κ ε)^(1/3) from Eq. (15) so that it is consistent in the convective limit. The radiative part of the surface heat flux that contributes to γ_T is the shortwave absorbed above depth h_I... see D6. Flux = -ν(∂zX - γ) (Eq. 9).
- **Application with time stepping**: γ enters the right-hand side vector H of the implicit solve (H_n = Δt/Δ_n [(ν γ)_{n+1/2} - (ν γ)_{n-1/2} + ...], D10a-c, l.2745-2765), i.e. it is *explicit* in the counter-gradient flux while ν is implicit. Velocities: H holds only Coriolis (time-centred) and bottom BC terms (D11-D12).
- **KPP and layers that move** (Hal04): HYCOM applies KPP on the hybrid layers at p-points: interfaces' diffusivities from the initial profiles, tri-diagonal system following L94, iterate, then mix; viscosity interpolated to u, v points (l.312-340). Hal04 report differences among KPP, GISS, M-Y "too small relative to other errors" (l.19-22) in low-resolution Atlantic runs. They also keep the surface momentum flux in layer 1 and the bottom flux over the bottom 10 m outside the mixing routine (l.303-306). No thin-layer/remap issue is documented for KPP in Hal04; the hybrid grid generator's minimum thickness d_k keeps the near-surface layers ≥ d_k (10 m in their runs, l.226-233), which makes the KPP columns coarse enough.
- **Resolution implication for pyOCEAN**: in the ALE hybrid, near-surface layers will often be thicker than in L94's tests, so the bias (h_d < h_c) and oscillation fixes of Appendix D are needed; and when layers are < 1 m Ri_c should be ≈ 0.25-0.3 (L94 l.1095-1104).

## D6. Surface heat flux, shortwave penetration, with these closures

- **Two-band exponential** (L94 Eq. A5, l.2060-2085): I(d) = I_0 Σ_{i=1}^{N_I} r_i exp(-d/μ_i), N_I = 2 "adequate for most physical models" (Paulson & Simpson 1977); Jerlov type I: r1 = 0.58, r2 = 0.42, μ1 = 0.35 m, μ2 = 23 m (l.2067-2069). Other Jerlov water types have their own r_i, μ_i (Paulson & Simpson 1977 table; **[verificar]** the values for IA, IB, II, III: type IA r=0.58, μ1=0.35, μ2=23; IB 0.67/1.0/17; II 0.77/1.5/14; III 0.78/1.4/7.9 — memory, not in the local text).
- **Interaction with the boundary-layer scheme** (L94 Appendix A, l.2020-2085): the surface buoyancy flux entering L and w* (and hence h) is B_f = B_f(nonsolar + the solar part absorbed within h_B); "setting h_B = 0 assumes no contribution; at most only radiation absorbed in the OBL has the potential to affect the turbulence, so h_B can be at most h" (A3, l.2043-2050); the radiative part contributing to the nonlocal heat flux γ_T is evaluated with an analogous depth h_γ (A4); the actual choices were h_B = h, h_γ = 0 (the "KPP simulation, with h_B = h, h_γ = 0", l.1274) after tests that showed "the treatment of solar radiation, through our values of h_B and h_γ, is reasonable" (Appendix C, l.2255-2263). The profile of the divergence of I(d) is added as a nonturbulent heating Q_n(d) = ∂I/∂d term in the heat equation (35c, D10: J_{n+1/2}, the nonturbulent forcing at interfaces).
- **HYCOM**: shortwave radiation, Jerlov (1976) two-band exponential, "is always used in the three differential models (KPP, GISS and MY) along with the PWP model"; surface heat and mass fluxes are distributed over layers inside each mixing algorithm; with Kraus-Turner it is optional (Hal04 l.292-301). Wind stress goes into layer 1 and bottom stress into the bottom 10 m before the mixing algorithm, which then mixes momentum (l.302-306).
- **For GLS k-ε** the surface buoyancy flux enters only as a flux boundary condition on the temperature equation (not in the TKE equation unless surface TKE injection is used, K18 l.664) and through B in the production terms; penetrating shortwave enters as a heating profile in the T equation, which changes N² and hence B. K18 l.457: "irradiance in water, I ... the positive vertical gradient of irradiance depends on ..." (K18 Eq. 16-17, l.450-470, l.1028-1030 on the zero surface salinity flux in a Lorenz grid).
- **Layer-integrated application on moving layers**: the heating of layer k is `(I(z_{k+1/2}) - I(z_{k-1/2}))/(ρ0 cp h_k)`, using the interface depths of the layers *at that time step*; sum conserves the heat exactly if the bottom flux is taken as the residual (not in the papers; trivial FV consequence).

## Idealised tests with numbers

1. **Kato-Phillips / Price (1979) wind-mixing experiment** (W05 §3.3, l.1620-1640; UB03 §3d, l.882-916). Linear initial stratification, constant surface stress, no rotation:
   `D_m = 1.05 u*_s N0^(-1/2) t^(1/2)` (W05 Eq. 60), u*_s = √(τ_s/ρ0), N0 = 0.01 s⁻¹ ("the initial buoyancy frequency"), t since the onset of stress.
   Warner et al. setup (Table 7): 5000 × 100 × 50 m basin with periodic E-W (effectively 1-D), 200 levels in the vertical (they list 15 × 5 × 200 grid points), τ_s = 0.1 N m⁻², T linear from 13 °C (surface) to 10 °C (bottom) with ρ0 = 1000 kg m⁻³, Δt = 30 s. Mixed-layer depth is where k < 1e-5 m² s⁻² (W05 l.1618). Findings: M-Y 2.5 over-predicts D_m by > 20 % because c3- = 0.9 is too large (W05 l.1651-1655; Eq. 47 c3- = 5.08 c1 - 4.08 c2 for the KC stability function), calibrated k-ε/k-ω/gen match Price. Trowbridge (1992) gives an analytical model matching Price's formula (W05 l.1635). Original: Kato & Phillips (1969), re-interpreted by Price (1979) (UB03 l.882).
   Calibration rule behind it (UB03 Eq. 28; W05 Eq. 44-47): steady homogeneous shear (P + B = ε) gives `Ri_st = (c_μ... )` as a function of c3-; adopting **Ri_st ≈ 0.25** reproduces Kato-Phillips for all calibrated two-equation models (UB03 l.910-916, l.1316-1318; Table 8: c3- = 2.62 (MY82), -0.629 (R87 k-ε), ... with the Canuto A ASM). Equivalent to W05 Eq. 47/48 relations of c3- to (c1, c2) (KC: c3- = 5.08 c1 - 4.08 c2 = -0.52 for k-ε).
   Test in pyOCEAN: 1-D column, ALE-σ and ALE-z* with and without regrid, expected D_m within ±5-10 % of Eq. 60 for t of hours; also use for the ALE remap of k, ε (D1): the run with the remap must not change D_m beyond the run without it.
2. **Open-channel flow (W05 case 1)**: log-layer velocity and length scale; parabolic ν_t profile; reference data Nezu & Rodi (1986) (W05 l.1500-1580, Fig. 3). Gives a boundary-layer test of the k, ψ flux boundary conditions.
3. **Convective boundary layer with KPP** (L94 §5, l.926-960, Table 1): motionless 35 PSU ocean, ∂zT = 0.1 °C m⁻¹, N = 0.016 s⁻¹, T_0 = 22 °C, cooling Q = -100 W m⁻² for 3 days; entrainment ratio β_T = -0.2 requires C_v = 1.5-1.6 (Table: C_v = 1.4/1.6/1.8 → β_T = -0.171/-0.206/-0.243). Resolution test in Appendix C: Δz = 0.2, 2.0, 3.33 m; boundary layer deepens 13.5 → 17.5 m in 2 days; mixed-layer temperature in the coarse run within 0.01-0.02 °C except 0.1 °C when h_m sits near a layer centre (Fig. C3). KPP test of shear-driven deepening: idealised seasonal thermocline N = 0.01 s⁻¹, mixed layer 17 m, u\* = 0.01 m s⁻¹, 5-m layers (Fig. C1-C2); h_c vs h_d plot; deepening h ∝ u*(t/N)^(1/2).
4. **LOTUS and OWS Papa** (L94 §6; 1-D with real forcing) — used by L94 to fix Ri_c = 0.3, resolution independence; not self-contained.
5. **Tracer variance decay with a closure** (H10 Eq. 35, K14, BR08): compare 2 K_v (∂z s)² against the numerical variance decay in a lock-exchange / internal seiche; H10 §4.1-4.2 (l.1018-1160): fully Lagrangian and N-adaptive grids reduce numerical mixing and "perform similarly" (l.1022). Already in `diagnostics/mixing.py`.

## Disagreements and caveats

- **P, B discretisation**: W05/ROMS (old K, old M², N²) vs Burchard (2002)/GOTM energy-conserving (new/old shear product). K18 flags energy-consistency as important but gives no quantification.
- **Ri_c**: 0.25 (fine grid) to 0.4 (Δz = 5 m) in L94 vs 0.3 as the single fixed choice (L94 l.1104) vs CVMix default 0.3 **[verificar]**.
- **Matching**: L94 matching (full) vs CVMix simple matching; not resolvable from the local text.
- **c3- for stable stratification**: k-ε: -0.52 (KC) / -0.63 (CA) / -0.57 (CB) from W05 Table 2; UB03 -0.629 for R87 with CA and Ri_st = 0.25 (consistent); generic: 0.05 (UB03, CA) vs 0.1 (W05 with KC).
- **k, ε location**: interfaces (GOTM/GETM, ROMS, MOM6 kappa-shear, CROCO) vs layer centres (HYCOM M-Y, Hal04) — each is self-consistent; interface placement keeps the Neumann BCs simple (K18 l.3071-3076).
- **Remap of k, ε**: no local paper treats remapping of ψ and k; HYCOM treats them as layer scalars; MOM6 reinitialises; Oceananigans carries TKE as a σ-weighted tracer (`tke_in_ale.md`). Treat as a design decision with a test (Kato-Phillips with and without regrid).
